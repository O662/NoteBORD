## Phase 3a: pen gestures and canvas tools — built, checked on the tablet (2026-09-29), with fixes after

The first half of Phase 3: selection and the pen gestures. Shapes, text boxes, stickies, frames and the Insert menu are Phase 3b.

**Tablet check, 2026-09-29.** Hold to straighten, select/lasso (pen, one and two fingers, corners, straighten lines), ruler, laser, zoomed use and palm rejection all worked. Fixed afterwards:
- Flick arrows: only 4 of 20 worked, some facing the wrong way. The tip is now found as the furthest point along the line (real S Pen tips are rounded, which failed the old sharp-corner test and the smoothness check right before the tip), hooks may curve and take up to 500 ms, and a start hook must angle away from the line (15–50°) and be at least 12 px, so a landing backswing no longer adds a head at the wrong end. On 500 simulated pen flicks: 97% become arrows; 0 plain lines (with or without a backswing) and 0 of 500 zig-zags do.
- Scribble to erase reacts twice as fast (Light 2, Normal 3, Firm 4 back-and-forths).
- A finger can now drag the rotate knob and corner handles.
- Ruler: pinch along it to lengthen or shorten it (past a 12% dead zone, so turning it doesn't resize it), cm / mm / inch ticks and numbers, the length of a line drawn along it in the chip, and a tap on the chip opens presets, a number pad (to 3 decimals, ±) and the unit.

Fixed in `lib/recognition/segment.dart`:
- A stroke joins the line it's level with *where it is* (the few strokes nearest it sideways), so lines that rise or fall stay whole; following straight on from the stroke before, it gets more leeway for tails and tall letters. ## Phase 6a: math mode — built; the model checked on Windows, not yet tried on the tablet

The owner's request (2026-10-15). Three recognizers were put to the owner: MyScript iink, ML Kit with our own parser, and an open model on the device. **The owner chose the open model**: it works fully offline, runs on Linux too, costs nothing, and can't be withdrawn by a vendor. Graphs you can trace, 3D, and the number line, unit circle and polar grid (Graph.png, the rest of MathKit.png) are the next part of Phase 6.

**Recognizer.**
- **The model.** CoMER (ECCV 2022) runs on the device through ONNX Runtime (`flutter_onnxruntime` 1.9.0), with no network and no account. It is the same on Android, iOS, Windows, Linux, macOS and web.
- **Size.** The weights ship with the app as 8-bit integers: `assets/math/`, 7.3 MB.
- **How it was built.** The network was re-implemented from the paper's code. The published checkpoint was loaded as tensors only, then exported as an encoder plus a step-by-step decoder that keeps its caches on the native side (`tool/math_model/`, with a README).
- **Accuracy.** 62.9% of CROHME 2014's handwritten expressions come out exactly right (the paper reports 59.3%); the 8-bit model scores 62.0% against 62.3% at full precision. On real pen strokes from Google's MathWriting it gets 36%, but that set is long Wikipedia formulas.
- **Licence: personal, non-commercial use.** The checkpoint was trained on CROHME (CC BY-NC-SA), and so is every open handwritten-math model checked. It must be replaced before Endless is sold. The app only needs two ONNX files with the same inputs (`assets/math/NOTICE.txt`, `tool/math_model/README.md`).

| Item | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Reading math | The selected strokes are drawn as the model expects: white on black, 3 px lines, writing 30 px tall, at most 256 × 1024 px. Then a beam search (10 wide) runs left-to-right and right-to-left in the same steps, and each answer is rescored by the other direction, as in CoMER. - Marking, forgetting, adding and deleting cards are item edits: undo steps, sealed with a locked page, carried by board files.
- **The index** (`index.sqlite`, schema 3) gains `study_items`, filled from the pages like the search rows. The Remember and Flashcards screens and the sidebar's counts read it. An open page reaches it about 0.3 s after a change. `indexVersion` 3 reads every notebook again once, from recognition.json, so marks made before show up.

## Phase 7b: charts linked to tables and Excel, and live items — built, not yet tried on the tablet

The owner's request (2026-10-27): charts bound to a table on the page or an Excel range that update live and can be unlinked into a snapshot (Charts.png), and live items: the date, an analog or digital clock, time stamps and checklists (LiveItems.png), with the Insert menu's Live widgets tiles wired. The calendar and planner are Phase 7c.

**How it's stored (docs/BOARD_FORMAT.md):**
- **A chart** is a `chart` item. It names its source by item id on the same page (a table, or an Excel card with a sheet and an optional range), and keeps a copy of the cells it read (`snapshot.cells`), which is what's drawn. A chart therefore draws in thumbnails, exports and other apps without its source being loaded.
- **Following the source** happens in the notebook's commands, like connectors. `_withCharts` runs next to `_withConnectors` on every edit: a table edited, an Excel card given a newer file, or a source renamed rewrites its linked charts **in the same undo step**. Deleting the source turns its charts into snapshots in that step too (Undo links them again).
- **Clocks and dates** are `widget` items (`clock`, `date`). **Time stamps** are `timestamp` items. **Checklists** are `checklist` items whose entries hold their handwriting, the way sticky notes do (`InkHolder`).
